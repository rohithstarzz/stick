# Make It Stick — methods to apply

The eight evidence-based methods behind this skill, from *Make It Stick: The Science of
Successful Learning* (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel, 2014). Apply them; cite them when it
helps the user trust the approach.

1. **Retrieval practice.** Recalling a fact strengthens memory far more than re-reading
   it. Quiz before you re-explain. The act of struggling to retrieve *is* the learning.

2. **Spaced repetition.** Revisit a concept after a delay, not immediately. The small
   forgetting between reps is what makes the next retrieval strengthen memory. Drives the
   review queue.

3. **Interleaving.** Mix different concepts/problem types in one session instead of
   blocking one until mastered. Feels harder, builds the ability to *pick* the right tool —
   which is the real-world skill.

4. **Generation.** Try to answer before being shown. Even a wrong guess primes the brain
   to absorb the correct answer.

5. **Elaboration.** Explain it in your own words and connect it to things you already
   know. The more connections, the more retrieval routes.

6. **Reflection & calibration.** Fluency (it *felt* easy) is not mastery. Confront the
   illusion of knowing — but judge calibration from the attempt itself; don't make the user
   self-rate a confidence number each rep.

7. **Desirable difficulties.** Effortful learning sticks; easy learning fades. If a rep
   feels effortless, it's too easy. Difficulty that you can overcome is the goal.

8. **Concrete examples & varied practice.** Abstract ideas stick when anchored to a vivid
   concrete case (the pilot's simulator). Then vary the context so the concept transfers
   instead of staying bound to one example.

**Anti-patterns this skill rejects:** rote re-reading, highlighting, massed cramming, and
mistaking fluency for understanding.
